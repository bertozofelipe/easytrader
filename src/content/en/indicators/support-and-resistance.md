**Support** is a price zone where declines tend to stop because buyers show up. **Resistance** is a zone where rallies tend to stop because sellers show up. They are not exact lines but **zones**.

## Why they work

- **Market memory:** people who bought at a price and watched the asset fall tend to sell when price gets back there, to "break even".
- **Clustered orders:** many traders place orders at obvious levels, such as previous highs and lows.
- **Round numbers:** levels like $100,000 on Bitcoin or 1.10 on EUR/USD attract attention.

## How to spot them

- Previous highs and lows, especially those tested several times.
- Areas where price "moved sideways" for a while.
- Key moving averages (such as the 200-period), which act as dynamic levels.
- Higher timeframes (daily, weekly) have more relevant levels than 5-minute charts.

## Role reversal

When resistance is broken decisively, it often becomes **support** — and vice versa. It is one of the most widely watched patterns in technical analysis.

## Caveats

- The more times a level is tested, the weaker it tends to become.
- False breakouts are common: price pushes past the level and quickly comes back.
- Define the zone with some margin rather than an exact price.
